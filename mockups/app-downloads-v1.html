Downloads, for watching offline

You asked whether members can download titles, open them from the tab bar, and keep watching offline for as long as the membership is paid. Yes. This is how it would look, what it can’t do, and the calls I need from you before building. Nothing is built yet.

Yes, in the app

  • It works in the app you already have. Downloading and offline playback use pieces that are already inside Expo Go, so you can try it on your iPhone before there is a store build.
  • The file comes from Cloudflare, signed like playback. Each title gets an MP4 copy there. The app fetches it with a link that lasts a day at most and that only a signed-in member can get.
  • The month rule lives on the phone. The phone keeps the date the paid month ends. Offline before that date, downloads play. After it, they lock until the phone connects once and the membership checks out.
  • A download costs you one full viewing. Cloudflare bills $1 per 1,000 minutes delivered. A 90-minute film downloaded once is about 9¢, watched or not.

What it can’t do

  • No downloads on the website. A browser can’t keep a protected file. Downloads are app-only, and the website would point people to the app.
  • It isn’t studio-grade copy protection. The file sits in the app’s own storage, where Photos and Files can’t see it, and the screenshot block still applies. But Cloudflare has no DRM, so someone with a hacked phone could pull the file out. Only a provider with DRM changes that, which is one more reason for the Bunny move.
  • One size only. Cloudflare makes a single MP4 per title and the app can’t ask for a smaller one, so a long film is a big file.
  • Swiping the app away pauses a download. It carries on while the app is open, and usually in the background on an iPhone, and picks up when the app is opened again.
  • Three things only your iPhone can confirm: that the downloaded file plays, that a paused download resumes instead of starting over, and how big Cloudflare’s files really are.

How it would look

Six screens, in the order a member meets them. The dates assume a membership that renews on Dec 14.

Six calls that are yours

Each has my pick. Say “go with your picks” or change any of them, and I’ll build from that.

1Which plans can download?

You said as long as they have the subscription. The open part is which plans. Offline there are no ads, so a Standard member, whose plan has ads, would be watching without them.

My pickPremium and Sponsor, plus anyone who bought the title. Standard sees “Downloads come with Premium”.
2What happens the day the month is over?

Until that date downloads play with no connection. After it they lock until the phone connects once, as you asked. A renewal sometimes lands a few hours late.

My pickLock two days after the date, so a late renewal locks nobody out. If the membership really ended, keep the files 30 days, then remove them. Say “no grace” and it locks on the date itself.
3Titles someone bought

Buy to own promises they keep the title, so a membership date shouldn’t lock it.

My pickNo membership needed. The phone still connects once every 30 days so a refund or a legal takedown can reach it.
4Do creators get a say?

Many of your creators chose you because their work isn’t handed out, and a download puts a copy on a member’s phone.

My pickA switch per title, “Allow downloads in the app”, on by default, that the creator can turn off. The AI policy page and the Terms each get a sentence, which adds a line for the lawyer.
5How many, on how many phones?

Without a cap one membership could fill several phones, and every download costs you minutes.

My pick25 titles per phone, 2 phones per account, Wi-Fi only unless they switch that off. Removing a download frees its slot.
6Where it sits in the tab bar

The bar has four tabs, each opening a short menu. A fifth tab would squeeze the other four on a small phone.

My pickFirst row of the Watch tab’s menu, as drawn, with the count beside it. Offline, the app opens straight onto Downloads.
