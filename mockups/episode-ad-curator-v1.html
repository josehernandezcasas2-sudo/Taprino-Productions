Episode Ad Curator — v1 mockups, revised with your answers

A screen every title passes through before it goes to the live scheduler (and that the on-demand player reads too). It takes the episode's length and the creator's break times, lets you compose each break out of pieces, and gives back one exact number: the air length. That number is what the scheduler has been missing.

Why the timelines go weird today. A creator types break times ("0:00, 10:00, 20:30"); each break plays one house ad of whatever length comes up. On a live channel the mid-roll breaks aren't played at all — the episode runs straight through and ads only fill the slot's tail and the gaps between shows. So a 27:40 title books a 28-minute slot, and the moment real breaks exist nothing knows how long they are. With a plan, a break is a fixed number of seconds, so 27:40 + 2:00 of breaks = 29:40, a 30-minute slot, with 20 seconds of bumpers to close.

Words used below. A break is a point in the show where ads play. A break is made of pieces: an exact section (15 s — any approved 15-second ad fills it at air time), a range section (10–15 s — holds one ad inside the range, books the top of the range, bumper takes the rest), a flex block (your "custom size": N seconds that takes ads of any length, best fit, and keeps the last few seconds for a bumper), a pinned ad (one specific creative, placed by hand), and a bumper ("presented by", "supported by", station ID — the auto one comes from a dropdown). A self-implemented ad is a sponsor segment already inside the video; you mark it so breaks keep clear of it and a QR / link card can show over it.

Decided so far (Oct 14). Admins and content schedulers curate. Advertisers are steered to the size list, and longer sizes can be added. No cap per break; under 15 minutes of ads per hour on a channel. Bumper time is computed, not typed: 1 s to 20 s or more, whatever makes a break an even length, and a dropdown picks which bumpers. Self-ads never count but are always shown. Pinning is internal: Studio Tapa's own ads only. Section 5 explains the two rules you asked about; section 10 lists what's still open.

2 · The pieces

what each kind of section does with its seconds

Exact section

15 s = any approved 15-second ad. The seconds are fixed; which ad plays is decided at air time by the weighted draw that already exists. If no 15-second ad is eligible that day, the fallback rule plays (bumper by default).

Sizes: 10 · 15 · 20 · 30 s to start; longer ones are added in settings. The advertiser form steers uploads to the list.
Range section

10–15 s books 15 seconds. It holds one ad whose length falls in the range (a 12-second ad, say) and bumper time takes the 3 seconds left, so the air length never moves. This is the home for ads that don't come in neat sizes.

Ranges: 10–15 · 15–20 · 20–30 s.
Flex block

60 s, any lengths. At air time the block is packed with eligible ads of any length, longest first; whatever is left to the even length (1 s to 20 s or more) is bumper time. Three sections placed side by side turn into one of these when the merge setting is on. Type any length for a custom block.

The piece you asked for: "a custom ad size that actually does that".
Pinned ad

One specific creative, as long as it is. Internal only: Studio Tapa's own ads (the shop, Post Puppeteers, Pitch Room, a channel promo) from the Ads tab. Advertisers' ads are never pinned; they fill sections through the draw.

The “Presented by” bumper takes its brand from the pinned ad in the break.

5 · Bumper time and empty sections

the two rules you asked me to explain

Bumper time · computed, never typed
Ads first, bumpers to the even number

A bumper is a short fixed clip: the station ID (5 s), the “Back in a moment” sting (4 s), “Presented by” (6 s). A break books an even length (rounded up to 15 s in the mocks). Ads are placed first, and whatever is left is bumper time: 1 s, or 20 s or more. The engine fits the longest bumpers it can; a remainder too short for any clip shows the hold card, the channel logo, silent.

Empty section · the day it airs
A section is a promise, not a file

“Exact 20 s” means “play some approved 20-second ad here”. Which one is drawn when the break actually plays, from ads that are approved, active, inside their dates, have credits, and allow Live TV / Main player. If no such ad exists that day, the 20 seconds still have to happen or the schedule drifts. So, in order:

The hour meter adds up breaks, gaps and tails per clock hour. Over 15:00 the hour turns red and the block that tipped it is flagged. Self-ads show in it as a note, uncounted.

Edit a plan after it's scheduled and the block keeps its length; if the air length changes, the scheduler flags the block ("plan is now 31:10") instead of silently moving the day.

Live channel and main player

The channel engine pauses the episode at 18:30, runs the 45 s plan (a drawn 15 s ad, the pinned Studio Tapa ad, then bumpers), and resumes. The slot math holds because the plan is exact. Someone tuning in mid-break sees the countdown card as now.

8 · Phones

same rule as the scheduler

The curator is a tablet / computer screen: dragging pieces onto a 30-minute bar needs the width. On a phone the Library row shows the plan's state and air length and says "open on a tablet or computer to curate", like /schedule does. The app's admin tab gets the same note; the self-ad marker in the creator's submission works on phones as it's a form.
9 · Things in the mocks you may not have thought of

or easy to add

  • A default plan for everything, so the scheduler never waits on curating: one exact 30 s section per creator break. Curating is editing, not unblocking.
  • Air length rounds to the minute and the curator shows the tail ("0:20 bumpers to close"), the same whole-minute rule the channel already uses, so what you see here is what the day column gets.
  • Eligible-ad counts on every piece ("14 ads"), so you see an empty section before the day it airs, not after.
  • Pinned pieces never go empty: they're Studio Tapa's own ads, which need no credits, so a pinned promo always plays.
  • "Presented by" takes the brand from the pinned Studio Tapa ad in the break; no typing, and a break without one falls back to the station ID.
  • Copy this plan to the whole series (same break times, same pieces) with one button; most series keep the same shape episode to episode.
  • Breaks keep clear of the self-ad and of each other (a minimum spacing setting), which is the podcast case where a creator types a break two minutes after a sponsor read.
  • The scheduler flags, never moves: editing a plan after scheduling changes the block's length only when you say so.
  • Per-channel bumper set: TapaTV's station ID on CH 01, another channel's on CH 02, chosen by the channel, not per episode.
  • Podcasts too: audio episodes get the same plan with audio ads and audio bumpers; the RSS feed keeps serving the file without them (it's a different product).
  • Preview a break in the curator: plays the pieces back to back with whatever would fill them today.
10 · What's decided, what's left

your answers, folded in

  • Decided · who curates: full admins and content schedulers. Creators set break times and mark self-ads; advertisers never see the screen.
  • Decided · sizes: advertisers are steered to the size list (10 / 15 / 20 / 30 s to start); longer sizes can be added in settings.
  • Decided · caps: none per break; under 15:00 of ads per clock hour on a channel, counted by the scheduler across breaks, gaps and tails.
  • Decided · bumper time: computed, 1 s to 20 s or more, whatever makes the break an even length; the dropdown picks which bumpers.
  • Decided · self-ads: never counted, always shown, with a warning when a break lands near one.
  • Decided · pinning: internal only, Studio Tapa's own ads. Advertisers fill sections through the draw.
Still open
  • Live mid-rolls for every title? Today the live channel plays a show straight through. With plans, it stops for each break. The mocks assume every title, default plan included (one 30 s exact section per creator break). The alternative: only hand-curated titles stop for breaks; default plans keep ads between shows only.
  • Round breaks to 15 s (the mocks), 10 s, or 30 s? This is what "even time" means in practice.
  • The fallback order in section 5: a Studio Tapa ad that fits, then bumpers, then the hold card. Yes, or a different order?
  • "Presented by" names the pinned Studio Tapa brand. Should it also name an advertiser whose ad was drawn in the break (free, TV-like), or never?
  • The hold card: the channel logo on the channel's colour, silent. Fine, or a short loop (the sting on repeat)?
